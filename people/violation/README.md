# Violations

A violation is a documented breach of the policies in this handbook or of
the commitments in your agreement. For interns, the ladder below applies.
For contractors and clients, remedies follow the signed agreement
(notice, cure and termination terms) rather than warnings; the record
keeping is the same.

1. **First violation**: a written warning from the supervising founder,
   stating exactly what happened and what changes.
2. **Repeated violations** of the same kind within a year: termination of
   the engagement, per the notice terms of your agreement.
3. **Severe misconduct** (harassment, dishonesty, forged documentation,
   breach of confidentiality) skips the ladder entirely: immediate
   termination, and where warranted, referral to law enforcement.

Examples that earn a violation:

- Committing to a client or peer meeting and missing it without telling the
  other party.
- Dishonesty in any form, including submitting fake documentation.
- Going silent on committed ceremonies or communication without notice.
- Careless handling of credentials or confidential material (see the
  [cybersecurity policy](../../policies/cyber/)).

Violations are recorded in writing and shared with the person involved:
nothing goes on the record silently.

## Who this applies to

Everyone covered by the [policies](../../policies/): the founders,
independent contractors and CueLABS™ interns. The ladder above applies to
interns as it is written. For contractors and clients, the signed agreement
sets the remedy. Founders are held to the same standards, and a concern
about a founder goes to the CEO or another founder: see
[how to report a concern](../../policies/#reporting-a-concern).

## The right to respond

Before a violation is recorded or a decision is made, the person involved is
told in writing what is alleged and is given a fair chance to respond, and
what they say is recorded with the finding. For severe misconduct the
chance is short and does not hold up interim measures, such as suspending
access while facts are gathered: see
[what happens after a report](../../policies/#what-happens-after-a-report).
A person who thinks a decision is wrong can ask a founder who was not
involved to look at it again, and can add their own account to the record.
Ask for that review within **14 days** of the decision. A founder who was not
involved reviews it and answers you within **14 days**.

## Non-retaliation and false reports

Nobody is penalised for raising a violation in good faith. Retaliation is
itself a violation. A report we find was knowingly false or made to harm
someone is dishonesty, which is severe misconduct under the ladder.

## The ladder does not limit other remedies

The ladder is how Cuesoft responds to intern conduct. It does not limit any
remedy, notice, cure, termination, payment or recovery right in a signed
agreement, or any right under the law. Where a signed agreement gives a
shorter or different route, that agreement is followed, and Cuesoft may
use more than one route for the same conduct. Termination terms for the
Websites and programmes are on the
[terms](https://terms.cuesoft.io/legal/termination/).

## Records and how long they are kept

Violations are recorded in writing, shared with the person involved, and
kept confidentially, with access limited to those who need it to act on the
record. Personal details in them are handled under the
[personnel privacy notice](../../policies/personnel-privacy/), and they are
kept for the periods in the privacy policy's
[retention page](https://privacy.cuesoft.io/handling/retention/): personnel
records are kept for the engagement and six years after it ends.

## Local law and changes

Local law applies on top of this page. Where it gives you a protection or a
right, that prevails over anything here that would reduce it. This page
changes the way the handbook changes: see
[changing a policy](../../policies/#changing-a-policy).
