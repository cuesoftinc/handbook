# Intellectual Property

Who owns what, in one page. The signed agreement for your engagement is
always the authority; this is the map of how the pieces fit.

## Work you do for Cuesoft

Work product created in connection with a Cuesoft engagement (code,
designs, inventions, documentation, improvements) is **assigned to
Cuesoft**: all rights, title and interest, including copyright, patent and
trade-secret rights. This is stated in every contractor and internship
agreement, and the assignment survives the engagement.

## The open-source products

For Apparule, Expendit and Upstat, Cuesoft publishes the assigned work
under the **MIT licence** in public repositories. That is the deliberate
other half of the assignment: contributors' commits ship under their own
GitHub identities, so interns and engineers leave with a public,
attributable record of everything they built: the git log is the CV.

## Client work

On CueHIRE™ engagements, intellectual property in the deliverables created
specifically for a client belongs (or transfers) to that client per the
engagement's terms, typically on full payment. Cuesoft retains its
pre-existing tools, frameworks, templates, generic know-how and reusable
libraries.

## Cueprise™ and company platforms

Cueprise™ (its software, source code, architecture, interfaces and
documentation) is and remains **exclusively Cuesoft's property**.
Licensees receive a limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable right to use
it; configurations and integrations done for a licensee do not transfer
ownership. Licensee data inside Cueprise™, however, remains **the
licensee's property**: see [data protection](../data-protection/).

## Trademarks

Cuesoft™, CueTA™, CueLABS™, CueHIRE™, Cueprise™, Cuelearn™, The CueShow™
and The CueBlog™ are Cuesoft marks. Use them exactly, with the mark, and never in a
way that implies endorsement of anything we have not endorsed.

## Who this applies to and what it covers

It applies to the founders, contractors, interns and anyone else who creates
or uses code, designs, content, data or ideas while acting for Cuesoft. It
covers everything created in connection with an engagement, everything you
bring into one, and Cuesoft's marks.

## Your duties

- **Assign and record.** Work product you create in connection with an
  engagement is assigned as the signed agreement says. Keep commits,
  designs and documents in company systems and repositories, not only on
  your own device.
- **Bring only what you may use.** Do not bring in code, designs, data,
  fonts, images or text that you do not have the right to use. This includes
  material from another employer, client, university or course, and
  anything under a licence that does not allow our use.
- **Respect licences.** Keep third-party and open-source notices intact, and
  tell the engagement lead or CTO before you add a dependency whose licence
  is not permissive or is not already used in the repository.
- **Your own earlier work.** Tell the engagement lead before you include
  your own pre-existing code or materials. Say what they are and under what
  licence you are giving them, in writing, so that ownership is clear.
- **AI tools.** Do not put confidential code, client material or personal
  data into an AI tool Cuesoft has not approved. You remain responsible for
  checking that AI-generated code or text can be used under its licence and
  is correct. See the [cybersecurity policy](../cyber/).
- **Feedback and ideas.** Ideas and suggestions you give about Cuesoft's
  products and services may be used by Cuesoft without obligation to you,
  except as the signed agreement says.
- **Marks.** Use the Cuesoft marks only as the paragraph above says and as
  the public [intellectual property terms](https://terms.cuesoft.io/legal/intellectual-property/)
  allow.

## What is not assigned

The programme and engagement terms set what is yours. For interns, work
created outside the internship's scope, on your own time and equipment, is
not assigned: see the
[CueLABS™ terms](https://terms.cuesoft.io/divisions/cuelabs/). For
contractors, work you create outside the engagement's scope, without
Cuesoft's time or materials, stays yours. Work created in connection with
the engagement is Cuesoft's, and you waive your moral rights in it where the
law allows. You may list engagement work in your portfolio unless it is
confidential. For everyone else, the signed agreement says.

## Reporting an infringement or a concern

If you think material infringes someone's rights, or that Cuesoft work
infringes another person's, tell [hello@cuesoft.io](mailto:hello@cuesoft.io)
or the COO at once, and if it concerns them the CEO or another founder: see
[how to report a concern](../#reporting-a-concern). To report content on a
Cuesoft website that infringes your own rights, follow the notice steps in
the public [intellectual property terms](https://terms.cuesoft.io/legal/intellectual-property/).
No one is penalised for a good-faith report, and a knowingly false one is a
violation.

## After a report, consequences and records

A founder who is not involved handles the report in confidence and may
suspend access or pause the work while the facts are gathered: see
[what happens after a report](../#what-happens-after-a-report). Taking or
using work you have no right to, or passing off company or client work as
your own, is a violation, and where it is deliberate it is severe
misconduct under [violations](../../people/violation/). That sits alongside
the remedies in the signed agreement and the law. Records are kept as the
[rules for every policy](../#records) say.

## Local law, signed agreements and changes

The signed agreement is the authority and prevails where this page differs.
Local law applies on top of this policy, and where mandatory law gives you a
right in your work, it prevails. The policy changes the way the handbook
does: see [changing a policy](../#changing-a-policy).
